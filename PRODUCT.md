# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Friends and family using Wished casually on mobile — browsing wishlists, creating their own wishes, and reserving gifts for others on the go. Primary context: quick glances between messages, spontaneous gift ideas, event planning. Secondary context: desktop for managing longer lists and editing.

## Product Purpose

Wished lets people create wishlists, share them with friends and family, and reserve gifts so nobody buys the same thing. Success looks like: someone opens the app, sees a friend's birthday is coming up, finds a wish, reserves it, and buys it — all in under a minute.

## Brand Personality

Warm, playful, generous. Like a gift-giving app should feel — the digital equivalent of wrapping paper and a handwritten card. Not corporate, not cold, not transactional. The tone is friendly and encouraging: "You found the perfect gift!" not "Item reserved successfully."

## Anti-references

- Cold, corporate SaaS dashboards (gray nav bars, metric cards, data-heavy)
- Over-designed "social" apps with infinite scroll and gamification
- Generic e-commerce product grids (prices, ratings, "add to cart")
- Anything that feels like a to-do list or project management tool

## Design Principles

1. **Generosity first** — Every interaction should feel like giving, not managing. Empty states encourage, errors apologize, success celebrates.
2. **Mobile-native, desktop-capable** — Design for thumb reach and glanceability first. Desktop is a comfortable expansion, not the primary canvas.
3. **Personality without decoration** — Warmth comes from copy, spacing, and micro-interactions, not from decorative UI elements. Every pixel earns its place.
4. **The gift is the star** — Wish photos and titles take priority over UI chrome. The interface steps back and lets the wishes shine.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Consider color blindness in gift-status indicators (don't rely on color alone for reserved/available). Support reduced motion.
